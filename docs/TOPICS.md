## Use the right starting technique

- **A motor or frame shakes:** collect position/current/vibration data; distinguish control underdamping, structural modes, backlash, saturation and timing before tuning.
- **A signal contains unexpected frequencies:** inspect sampling, sensor decimation and filtering; use a spectrum/Fourier analysis and controlled rate changes.
- **Several axes interact:** define frames, derive/fix the model, check coupling/controllability/observability, then compare cascade/decoupling/state-space methods.
- **A robot arm cannot reach a pose:** use FK/IK and workspace/singularity analysis before changing motors or controller gains.
- **Runtime is too short:** calculate energy under the mission load, including efficiency/mass/thermal effects, then measure the dominant losses.
- **A prototype works but is hard to produce:** review tolerances/datums, material/process, assembly, calibration/test, yield and configuration traceability.
- **The business case seems attractive:** test demand, actual unit cost, support/returns, yield uncertainty and payment timing before treating margin as profit or cash.

## Learning progression

Start with units, diagrams and the physical model needed for the task. Add transforms and classical control for a single-axis loop; add linear algebra/state space for coupled systems; add nonlinear/optimisation/learning methods when model behaviour or constraints justify them. Use [worked examples](../skills/mechatronics-engineering/references/worked-examples.md) and [browser experiments](BROWSER-SIMULATION.md) to connect ideas to visible results.

The [sources guide](../skills/mechatronics-engineering/references/sources.md) identifies primary learning and verification resources. Advanced topics are decision guides with equations, examples and limitations; a specialised design may still require deeper sources, measured parameters and domain experts.
